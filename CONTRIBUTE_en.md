# How to send messages — for contributors

> **TRANSLATION, FOR REVIEW ONLY.** The operative document is `CONTRIBUTE_vi.md`, in Vietnamese,
> which is the only version contributors are shown. This English text exists so that an ethics
> committee or a reviewer who does not read Vietnamese can check what contributors were told to
> do. Where the two differ, the Vietnamese governs, and a difference is a defect in this file.
>
> **DRAFT, NOT IN USE.** The redaction tool described here has not been written, and the `[…]`
> blanks are not filled. Hand this out only with the consent form (`CONSENT_vi.md`), and only once
> ethics approval exists.

Read the consent form first. This guide says only **how**; **why**, and **your rights**, are in the
form.

There are two ways to send:

- **Way 1 — copy and paste (preferred), sections 2–5.** You copy each message, paste it into a web
  page running on your own phone, review the redacted version, and send one file. The original text
  never leaves the phone, and the text is kept character for character.
- **Way 2 — send screenshots, section 6.** For messages you cannot copy. You crop the image, black
  out your own details, and send one `.zip` file. With this way the researcher sees the image exactly
  as you sent it.

No message needs retyping.

---

## 1. Which messages to send

Look at the **sender** shown at the top of the conversation:

| sender shown | example | send? |
|---|---|---|
| a brandname (letters) | `VCB`, `DAIHOC-X`, `TB-QUA` | **send** |
| a service shortcode (3–5 digits) | `8079`, `9029` | **send** |
| a full phone number (10 digits, starting `0` or `+84`) | `09xx xxx xxx` | **do not send** |
| a name in your contacts | `Mẹ`, `Lan lớp A` | **do not send** |

**A message from a personal phone number is not sent, whatever it says** — even when it is plainly a
scam. Many scam messages come from personal numbers; we know, and still do not collect them, because
the person behind that number has not consented and we have no way to ask them.

From brandnames and shortcodes we ask for both kinds:

- **scam, impersonation and spam** messages;
- **genuine messages sent by a system**: one-time codes, balance notifications, carrier, school and
  application notices.

Unsure about a message? **Leave it out.**

## 2. Before you start

1. Open the redaction page in your phone's browser: `[…]`
2. Once the page has loaded, **turn on airplane mode**. The page keeps working, because it sends and
   receives nothing over the network. Airplane mode is how you can see that for yourself.
3. Keep the page open. You will switch between the messaging app and this page.

## 3. Taking each message

**Copy the message in your messaging app:**

- **Android** (Google Messages, Samsung Messages…): long-press the message and tap the **Copy** icon
  (two overlapping squares) or the word *Copy*.
- **iPhone**: long-press the message bubble and tap **Copy**.

Copy **the whole message**, not a selected part of it.

**Paste it into the redaction page**, then fill four fields:

| field | what to enter | example |
|---|---|---|
| Sender | exactly as shown on the phone | `VCB-Bank` |
| Sender type | brandname / shortcode / unknown | brandname |
| Month received | month and year, **no day** | `03/2027` |
| You think this message is | genuine / spam / scam | scam |

The date is shown next to the message in the app. We ask only for the month, not the day or time: a
day and a time, together with the sender, could let someone who knows you guess the message is yours.

The last field is your opinion and **need not be certain**. Two annotators read every message again,
independently.

## 4. Reviewing the redacted text — the most important step

The page automatically replaces phone numbers, e-mail addresses and long digit runs (account and
card numbers, one-time codes) with tags such as `<PHONE>`, `<EMAIL>`, `<ACCOUNT>`, `<OTP>`. **Links
are kept as they are**, because they matter most for recognising a scam; the page masks only a phone
number, e-mail address or name inside a link. It cannot recognise people's names or addresses by itself: **you select them and tap the
mask button**.

An example, before and after redaction:

```
Before: Chao Nguyen Van A, tai khoan 0123456789 cua ban se bi khoa. Xac minh tai http://xacminh-taikhoan.example/k?sdt=0912345678
After:  Chao <NAME>, tai khoan <ACCOUNT> cua ban se bi khoa. Xac minh tai http://xacminh-taikhoan.example/k?sdt=<PHONE>
```

Read the redacted text and ask yourself: **is anything left that shows this message is mine?** In
particular:

- your name or a relative's;
- account, card, student, ID card or vehicle numbers;
- a home, rented room or dormitory address;
- **inside a link**: your name, phone number or customer code (e.g. `…?ma_kh=…`) — mask that part,
  or leave the message out if you cannot tell which part is yours;
- any detail you would not want others to read about you.

Mask anything left over. If you cannot, or still feel uneasy, **leave the message out**. The page
saves a message only after you mark it as reviewed.

**Do not correct spelling.** Missing diacritics, misspellings, odd capitals and strange characters
stay exactly as they are. Those "ugly" details are what the research measures.

## 5. Sending

1. Repeat sections 3–4 for each message. Send as many as you like; there is no minimum.
2. Tap **Download file** on the page. The phone saves a `.csv` file holding only **redacted**
   messages.
3. Turn airplane mode off and send the file through `[…]`. That channel does not record who you are.
4. Delete the `.csv` file from your phone once it is sent.

## 6. Way 2: sending screenshots

Only for messages you **cannot copy**. The rule in section 1 still holds: no screenshots of messages
from personal numbers.

1. **Take a screenshot** with the message open.
2. **Crop it** (the *Edit* / *Crop* tool built into the Photos app) so that only these remain:
   - the sender's name or shortcode at the top of the conversation;
   - that one message.

   Crop away the status bar (time, battery, carrier), the other messages above and below, and any
   notification shown over the screen.
3. **Black out** anything that points to you: names, account, card and student numbers, addresses…
   (the list in section 4). Use a black marker, **solid, 100% opacity**, until nothing can be read.
   Do not use blur effects or a highlighter: the text underneath can still be recovered.
4. **Review** the cropped, blacked-out image. If unsure, drop it.
5. **Name the image** after what you think the message is (optional): `that_01.png` (genuine),
   `rac_01.png` (spam), `luadao_01.png` (scam). Do not put your name in the file name.
6. **Compress** all images into one `.zip` file and send it through `[…]`, as in section 5. After
   sending, delete the `.zip` file and the screenshots.

What you should know about this way: the researcher sees the image **exactly as you sent it**,
including anything you did not black out. The researcher records only the **month** received if the
image shows a date, turns the image into text, masks the text, and deletes the image. Images are
never published.

An example of an acceptable image: `VCB-Bank` at the top, one message bubble below reading
`Tai khoan cua quy khach se bi khoa. Xac minh tai …`, and nothing else.

## 7. What not to do

- **Do not send uncropped, un-blacked-out screenshots** (section 6). A full-screen image holds more
  than the message: the time, the carrier, the other messages in the conversation, notifications
  from other apps.
- **Do not forward messages through Zalo, Messenger, e-mail or cloud notes** to move them to another
  device. That puts the unredacted text on another company's servers.
- **Do not tap links** in scam messages, not even to "have a look".
- **Do not send someone else's messages** — ones borrowed from a friend's or relative's phone. Send
  only messages on your own phone.

## 8. If something goes wrong

| problem | what to do |
|---|---|
| The messaging app will not let you copy | Use way 2 (section 6). Do not retype it. |
| A long message arrived split into parts | Copy each part and paste them into the same field, in order. |
| Unsure whether the sender is a personal number or a service | Leave the message out. |
| You sent a message you had not redacted well | Contact `[…]` before the publication date in the consent form and describe the message; you need not say who you are. |
