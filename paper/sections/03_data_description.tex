Redaction placeholders that may appear in \texttt{text} are
\texttt{<NAME>}, \texttt{<PHONE>}, \texttt{<ACCOUNT>}, \texttt{<OTP>}, \texttt{<EMAIL>},
\texttt{<AMOUNT>}, \texttt{<ADDRESS>}, \texttt{<ID>}, \texttt{<DATE>}, \texttt{<TIME>} and
\texttt{<TRANSACTION\_ID>}. Everything else in \texttt{text} is as received: odd punctuation,
capitalisation, missing diacritics, typos, unusual Unicode and filter-evading spellings are kept
exactly, because they are among the strongest signals a smishing message carries. Rows with
\texttt{capture = screenshot} are OCR transcriptions and may have normalised such spellings; a
study of them should use \texttt{paste} rows only.

A valid file satisfies six rules, each checked by the publishing script rather than assumed:
(1) \texttt{message\_id} is unique; (2) \texttt{text} contains no digit run of four or more
outside a placeholder, a link or an amount, and no \texttt{@}; (3) \texttt{final\_label} is
never \texttt{uncertain} or empty; (4) every message sharing a \texttt{template\_id} shares a
\texttt{split}; (5) the four \texttt{has\_*} flags are derived from the published patterns,
never typed; (6) \texttt{sender\_type} is never a phone number.

\FILL{T} templates at the primary threshold ($\tau = 0.8$), \FILL{$T_{0.7}$} and
\FILL{$T_{0.9}$} at $\tau = 0.7$ and $0.9$. Class balance: \FILL{legitimate / spam / phishing
counts and shares}. Distinct malicious (\texttt{spam} $+$ \texttt{phishing}) templates:

\subsection*{\texttt{sms\_external\_qavn.csv}: the external benchmark}
The rows of the Quality-Assured Vietnamese SMS Phishing Dataset~\cite{qavn_sms} (CC BY 4.0),
mapped into this corpus's placeholder vocabulary and held out as an independent test set. It is
\emph{not} part of \texttt{sms\_dataset.csv}, is \emph{not} annotated by this project, and
carries the source's own binary label. Its columns: \texttt{message\_id} (a \texttt{QAV\_} id
that traces to the source row); \texttt{text} (the source text with placeholder tokens remapped,
\texttt{[MONEY]} $\to$ \texttt{<AMOUNT>} and so on; \texttt{[TB]}, \texttt{[QC]} and brand
prefixes are message text and left alone); \texttt{label\_source} (\texttt{benign} /
\texttt{scam}, the source's 0/1 label, unchanged, the evaluation label); \texttt{template\_id},
\texttt{sender\_type} and the four \texttt{has\_*} flags, computed as for the contributed
corpus. It has no \texttt{final\_label}, no annotator columns and no train split, because none
were produced for it. \texttt{<NUMBER>} appears only in this file, as the mechanical image of
the source's \texttt{[NUMBER]} and \texttt{[POINT]} tokens, which collapsed phones, shortcodes
and quantities into one; \texttt{has\_phone} stays 0 on such a row.

\paragraph{Size.} 2{,}676 messages (1{,}907 \texttt{benign}, 769 \texttt{scam}), after 315 of
the source's 2{,}991 rows were dropped by redaction rule (2); most carried an unmasked one-time
code. Templates: \FILL{count at $\tau = 0.8$}. No external template coincides with any training
template of the contributed corpus (checked by the grouping script).

\subsection*{What is withheld}
\texttt{participant\_id} is kept privately and does not ship: with a handful of contributors it
is pseudonymisation, not anonymisation, since someone who knows the group could read off which
bank or school each uses. It exists so the leave-one-contributor-out result can be computed,
and that result is reported without the mapping. Raw text and screenshots never ship;
screenshots were deleted once their transcription was checked, before publication. No handset
or SIM identifier, date of birth or contact name was collected.
